En-Kai (Debby) Chang

I'm a master's student at National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, Taiwan, and a research assistant in the Critical Creative Computing Lab at National Cheng Kung University, advised by Prof. Ying-Yu Chen. Outside of research, I'm an amateur portrait photographer who loves hand-crafting and zine-making — and I bring that same hands-on, people-first curiosity into my work.

My work focuses on how underrepresented communities make, share, and contest meaning through technology, and I love working closely and collaboratively with the communities I study. I use qualitative methodologies, including co-design, ethnography, and research-through-design, to study care, identity, and domestic life.

Going forward, I'm interested in developing creative, asset-based technologies that build on communities' existing strengths and ways of making.

About

Research focus

My work sits at the intersection of critical computing and community-based design, working alongside underrepresented groups and families in non-Western contexts to understand how they make sense of, and push back against, the technologies shaping their lives.

Recent projects include supporting hermeneutical autonomy for LGBTQ+ people in Taiwan (CHI 2026), studying how families collectively make data with a shared domestic object to mediate care (TAICHI 2026 · Best Paper), and co-designing storytelling chatbots with Southeast Asian immigrant mothers (TAICHI 2025 · Honorable Mention). I'm applying to HCI PhD programs starting in 2027, hoping to extend this work — grounded in qualitative and design-research methods including research-through-design, co-design, and participatory design — toward questions of embodiment, gender, culture, and more-than-human relations in computing.

Current Work

Ongoing projects

Collaborator

Co-designing Storytelling Chatbots with Southeast Asian Immigrant Women

Advisor: Ying-Yu Chen, National Cheng Kung University

Co-design workshops on storytelling chatbots exploring how Southeast Asian immigrant women narrate their life stories within trustless and silencing social environments in Taiwan, followed by field deployment to inform design recommendations.

Co-Lead

Collective Data-Making for Family Care through a Domestic Object

Advisor: Yu-Ting Cheng, National Taiwan University of Science and Technology

A research-through-design project using a data-enabled design approach to extend prior work (TAICHI 2026, Best Paper) on how families and a shared domestic object collectively make and interpret data to mediate care relationships between members.

Project Lead

Short-Video Entrepreneurship Among Southeast Asian Immigrant Women in Taiwan

Advisor: Ying-Yu Chen, National Cheng Kung University

A critical computing study combining digital ethnography and interviews to examine how Southeast Asian immigrant women leverage personal assets to build entrepreneurial ventures on short-video platforms, and how they negotiate tensions between platform demands and domestic labor — surfacing design opportunities for platform design.
